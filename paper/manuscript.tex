{\Large A conditional Lean formalization of an $88.45\%$\\[3pt]

An explicit two-component mollifier\\[6pt]
10 November 2026 --- Revised manuscript for expert review

\begin{abstract}
We describe a Lean derivation of a fixed asymptotic proportion strictly
greater than $88.45\%$ for the zeros of the derivative of the Riemann
xi-function on the critical line, counted with analytic multiplicity.
The result has two explicit hypotheses: a uniform incomplete
inverse-phase estimate and a four-length Deshouillers--Iwaniec estimate.
We give their full parameter ranges, coefficient restrictions and
quantifier order. They are theorem arguments, not estimates proved by
this development. From these inputs the formal chain derives the
shifted moment for a height-dependent seven-vector coefficient family,
including all 49 ordered pairs and the actual approximate functional
equation remainders. It then connects the density-oriented arithmetic
main term to a fixed two-component mollifier, a kernel-checked rational
gate, and the actual xi-prime counting functions. A separate Python
interval calculation supplies a sharper numerical check but is not
trusted by Lean. We distinguish formal deduction, correspondence with
the cited analytic inputs, and independent mathematical review.
No simple-zero, optimality or publication-priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The statement and its trust boundary}\label{sec:statement}

Write
\[
 \xi(s)=H(s)\zeta(s),\qquad
 H(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\]
Let $\NT(X)$ be the total xi-prime zero count in the positive-height
window $(0,X]$, and $\NC(X)$ its critical-line subcount. Both use analytic
multiplicity. More precisely, these are the counts
\texttt{Zeta23.XiPrime.Ncount 0 X} and
\texttt{Zeta23.XiPrime.N0 0 X} in the pinned Alp\"oge--Furman (AF)
library; they are not its simple-zero counts. The formal counting
normalization is used throughout this manuscript, rather than replacing
the denominator by the zeta zero count.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional critical-line proportion]\label{thm:main}
Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:weil} and~\ref{hyp:di} below. There exist real
numbers $b>0.8845$ and $X_0$ such that, for every real $X\ge X_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 b\,\NT(X)\le\NC(X).
\end{equation}
In particular, the denominator is eventually positive and
$\NC(X)/\NT(X)>0.8845$ for all sufficiently large real $X$.
Consequently the asymptotic lower proportion is strictly greater than
$0.8845$, with a fixed positive margin.
\end{theorem}

The first assertion is
\lean{XiPrime8845.WuProportion.strict_proportion}; the quotient assertion
is \lean{WuProportion.ratio_eventually_gt}. The proof obtains a fixed
margin before allowing the terminal height to grow. It does not merely
prove a strict inequality whose margin could tend to zero.

The two hypotheses are the propositions
\lean{PRZZKloostermanInputs.WeilIncomplete} and
\lean{PRZZKloostermanInputs.DeshouillersIwaniecLemma1}.
No proof term for either proposition is supplied here. In particular,
the name Weil does not suppress the completion, prime-power and
composite-modulus obligations specified below.
The former public PRZZ witness axiom has instead been replaced by a
theorem deduced from these two arguments. The mathematical implication
proved by Lean and the validity of the external analytic specifications
are separate matters.

The construction uses Wu's argument-variation strategy \cite{Wu},
the long-mollifier moment architecture of
Pratt--Robles--Zaharescu--Zeindler \cite{PRZZ}, and the pinned AF
analytic/counting infrastructure \cite{AF,Repo}. The following sections
give a proof guide to the current source, not a claim that a short prose
argument replaces every contour, integrability and boundary lemma in
that source.

\section{The two explicit exponential-sum hypotheses}\label{sec:inputs}

Put $e(x)=\exp(2\pi\ii x)$. If $(n,q)=1$, let $\bar n_q$ be the least
nonnegative representative of the inverse of $n$ modulo $q$.
It is a modular inverse, never the real reciprocal. At modulus $1$
the representative is $0$, as in the Lean definition using
$\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$. All occurrences below are guarded by the relevant
coprimality conditions.

\subsection{The incomplete Weil input}

For $q\ge1$, $a\in\mathbb Z$, and $M,H\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, set
\[
 S_q(a;M,H)=
 \sum_{\substack{M<n\le M+H\\(n,q)=1}}
     e\!\left(\frac{a\bar n_q}{q}\right).
\]
\begin{hypothesis}[Uniform incomplete inverse-phase estimate]\label{hyp:weil}
For every real $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $C_\varepsilon>0$ such that,
for every integer $q\ge1$, every $a\in\mathbb Z$, and all nonnegative
integers $M,H$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weil}
 |S_q(a;M,H)|
 \le C_\varepsilon q^{1/2+\varepsilon}
          \gcd(|a|,q)\left(1+\frac Hq\right).
\end{equation}
\end{hypothesis}

Thus $C_\varepsilon$ is chosen \emph{before} $q,a,M,H$. There is no
assumption that $(a,q)=1$, that $q$ is prime, or that $H\le q$.
The gcd is to the first power in this precise contract; replacing it
by $1$ would change the hypothesis. The factor $1+H/q$ retains long
intervals. The case $H=0$ is the empty sum. This is an unweighted input;
smooth weights and their norms are supplied later.

For comparison, the separate definition \lean{WeilPrime} asks for
\[
 \left|\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}e\!\left(\frac{an+b\bar n_p}{p}\right)\right|
 \le 2\sqrt p
 \quad(p>2\text{ prime},\ (a,p)=(b,p)=1).
\]
This is the prime-field statement associated with Weil
\cite{Weil,Harcos}. It is not the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Deriving \eqref{eq:weil} from that statement requires estimates at prime
powers, Chinese-remainder assembly and completion of intervals, including
the logarithmic/divisor losses absorbed by $q^\varepsilon$. The
repository deliberately does not assert a proved
$\lean{WeilPrime}\Rightarrow\lean{WeilIncomplete}$ implication.
PRZZ invokes an incomplete estimate in its Type I argument
\cite[p.~21]{PRZZ}; Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:weil} records the exact
completion specification consumed here.

For an explicit classical route, use the composite-modulus estimate
\cite[Corollary~11.12, equation~(11.16)]{IK}:
\[
 |S(h,a;q)|\le\tau(q)q^{1/2}\gcd(h,a,q)^{1/2}.
\]
Put $g=\gcd(|a|,q)$. For an interval $J$ of fewer than $q$ consecutive
integers, finite Fourier inversion gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n\in J\\(n,q)=1}}e(a\bar n_q/q)
 =\frac1q\sum_{h\bmod q}
       \left(\sum_{n\in J}e(-hn/q)\right)S(h,a;q).
\]
The normalized sum of absolute values of the interval Fourier
coefficients is $O(\log(2q))$, so this is
$O(\tau(q)\sqrt{qg}\log(2q))$.
Each complete period is the Ramanujan sum $c_q(a)$. By von Sterneck's
formula $c_q(a)=\mu(q/g)\varphi(q)/\varphi(q/g)$ and
$\varphi(q)\le g\varphi(q/g)$, its modulus is at most $g$.
Splitting $H$ into complete periods and one such interval therefore
gives
\[
 |S_q(a;M,H)|\ll_\varepsilon
 q^{1/2+\varepsilon}g^{1/2}(1+H/q).
\]
Here $g\le\sqrt{qg}$ and the divisor/logarithmic loss is absorbed into
$q^\varepsilon$; $q=1$ is immediate. This stronger estimate implies
\eqref{eq:weil}. Thus the first-power gcd is a permissible weakening,
not a necessary cost of the zero mode. This completion argument is
supplied in prose, not as a Lean proof term for Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:weil}.

\subsection{The Deshouillers--Iwaniec input}

Let $C,D,U,V\ge1$ be real numbers, $\rho\ge1$ an integer, and
$b:\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\to\mathbb C$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:di-sum}
 \mathscr S(C,D,U,V;\rho,b)=
 \sum_{1\le c\le\lfloor C\rfloor}
 \ \sum_{\substack{1\le d\le\lfloor D\rfloor\\(c,\rho d)=1}}
 \left|
 \sum_{1\le u\le\lfloor U\rfloor}
 \ \sum_{\substack{1\le v\le\lfloor V\rfloor\\(v,c)=1}}
 b(u,v)e\!\left(\frac{u\,\overline{\rho vd}_c}{c}\right)
 \right|.
\end{equation}
In particular, the absolute value is \emph{inside} the two outer sums.
The outer and inner coprimality restrictions guarantee that $\rho vd$
is invertible modulo $c$. There is no coprimality restriction on $u$.
Set
\begin{align}
 \mathscr B_\varepsilon(C,D,U,V;\rho)
 ={}&(CDUV)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\bigl\{(CD)^{1/2}
       +(U+V)^{1/4}\mathscr R^{1/4}\bigr\},\label{eq:di-bound}\\
 \mathscr R={}&CD(U+\rho V)(C+\rho V^2)+\rho UV^2D^2.\notag
\end{align}

\begin{hypothesis}[Uniform DI four-length estimate]\label{hyp:di}
For every real $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $K_\varepsilon>0$ such that,
for every $C,D,U,V\ge1$, every integer $\rho\ge1$, and every complex
array $b$ satisfying
\[
 |b(u,v)|\le1\quad
 (1\le u\le\lfloor U\rfloor,\ 1\le v\le\lfloor V\rfloor),
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:di}
 \mathscr S(C,D,U,V;\rho,b)
       \le K_\varepsilon\,\mathscr B_\varepsilon(C,D,U,V;\rho).
\end{equation}
\end{hypothesis}

The unit bound holds on the full rectangular coefficient set, including
indices not used by a particular coprimality filter. No smoothness or
factorization of $b(u,v)$ is assumed. One array is used across all
$(c,d)$ in \eqref{eq:di-sum}; an arbitrary array depending on $c,d$ would
be a different input. Most importantly, the quantifiers are
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists K_\varepsilon>0\
 \forall C,D,U,V\ge1\ \forall\rho\ge1\ \forall b.
\]
The constant cannot depend on $\rho$, the four lengths, or the
coefficient array. Real upper bounds are implemented by natural floors.

The source specified for this contract is Deshouillers--Iwaniec,
\emph{Power mean-values for Dirichlet's polynomials and the Riemann
zeta-function, II}, Lemma~1, p.~310 \cite{DI}.
The publisher lists volume~43 (1984), pp.~305--312; the volume~48 printed
in PRZZ's reference is not used here. Inspection of the original
scanned Lemma~1 confirms the displayed inverse phase, the two
coprimality filters, and the four-length expression.
The source writes its coefficient array as \(c(u,v)\); here it is
renamed \(b(u,v)\) to distinguish it from the outer index \(c\).
The symbols
$(C,D,U,V,\rho,b(u,v))$ agree with the code's argument names.
Checking the source statement, its uniformity and this precise
translation is an external specification-review task, not an additional
theorem silently asserted by the axiom audit.

\subsection{How the inputs enter the proof}

The definitions in \lean{PRZZKloostermanInputs.lean} are propositions,
not declarations of analytic axioms. Intermediate modules use a class
\lean{PRZZProvedInput.DeepInputs} with exactly two fields, one for each
hypothesis. No default instance asserts those fields. The four headline
theorems expose them as separate ordinary arguments.

Type I partial summation uses \eqref{eq:weil}; the coupled Type II
estimate uses \eqref{eq:di}. Cutoff seminorms, arithmetic coefficients,
dyadic multiplicities, and the time/Mellin masses are not part of either
input's hidden constant. They are retained and estimated in the
adapters. This distinction is necessary for the height-dependent
profiles below.

\section{Fixed data and the height-dependent coefficient family}\label{sec:witness}

Fix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constants}
 R=1.04440033,\qquad \theta=\frac47-10^{-6},\qquad
 L=\log T,\quad y=T^\theta,\quad a=\frac12-\frac RL.
\end{equation}
The operator and profiles are
\begin{align}
q(x)={}&1-2.27043254x+0.304358115x^2+0.16793368x^3\notag\\
 &\hspace{6mm}+4.34152016x^4-5.3105844x^5+1.7701948x^6,\label{eq:q}\\
P_1(x)={}&-0.30528514x+2.50038954x^2-1.12177152x^3
                 -0.07333288x^4,\label{eq:p1}\\
P_2(x)={}&1.89779072x+0.56594804x^2+0.07023055x^3.\label{eq:p2}
\end{align}
Every finite decimal here is an exact rational number.
In particular $q(0)=P_1(1)=1$ and $P_1(0)=P_2(0)=0$.
For $1\le n\le y$ put $x_n=\log(y/n)/\log y$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coeff}
 a_n(T)=\mu(n)P_1(x_n)
       +\frac{(\mu*\Lambda*\Lambda)(n)}{(\log y)^2}P_2(x_n);
\end{equation}
put $a_n(T)=0$ outside this range. Here $*$ is Dirichlet convolution.
There is no squarefree restriction and no extra $2!$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi}
 \Psi_T(s)=\sum_{1\le n\le\lfloor y\rfloor}a_n(T)n^{-s-R/L},
 \qquad U_T(s)=q(-L^{-1}\partial_s)\zeta(s).
\end{equation}
Thus $\Psi_T(a+\ii t)=\sum a_n(T)n^{-1/2-\ii t}$.
The differential operator acts on zeta, not on the mollifier coefficients.

The elementary estimates $|\mu(n)|\le1$ and
$|(\mu*\Lambda*\Lambda)(n)|\le(\log n)^2$ give
$|a_n(T)|\le6.53474839$ for $T\ge2$. This coefficient bound alone does
not assert a moment theorem for arbitrary bounded coefficient arrays.
The relevant structured family has seven basis vectors:
\[
 3\mu(n)x_n^k\quad(1\le k\le4),\qquad
 3\,\frac{(\mu*\Lambda*\Lambda)(n)}{(\log y)^2}x_n^k
       \quad(1\le k\le3),
\]
all with the same original support indicator. Denote them by
$b_i(\theta,T;n)$, $0\le i<7$. The witness coordinates $z_i$ are the
seven coefficients in $P_1,P_2$, divided by $3$; hence $|z_i|\le1$.
The identity $a_n(T)=\sum_i z_i b_i(\theta,T;n)$ is exact at every
height. Both the logarithmic basis and its support depend on $T$.
The proved envelopes are $|b_i|\le6$ and
$|\sum_i z_i b_i|\le84$ for the larger box $|z_i|\le2$.

\section{Uniform moments from the two inputs}\label{sec:moment}

For coefficient sequences $c,d$, write
$D_c(s)=\sum_{1\le n\le N}c_n n^{-s}$ and define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_T(c,d;A,B)
 =\int_T^{2T}&\zeta(\tfrac12+\alpha+\ii t)
              \zeta(\tfrac12+\beta-\ii t)\notag\\[-2pt]
 &{}\cdot D_c(\tfrac12+\ii t)\overline{D_d(\tfrac12+\ii t)}
                     \Phi(t/T)\dd t,\label{eq:moment-pair}
\end{align}
where $\alpha=A/\log T$, $\beta=B/\log T$ and $\Phi$ is a fixed real
$C^\infty$ function with support in $[1,2]$. Positivity is not required
for this mixed-moment statement. It is required when positive moments
are used for counting.

For positive integers $m,n$, put $g=(m,n)$ and $\ell=[m,n]$ and set
\begin{align}
 \mathcal K_T(m,n;A,B)
 ={}&\frac1\ell\,\frac{g^{\alpha+\beta}}{m^\beta n^\alpha}
 \int_T^{2T}\left\{\zeta(1+\alpha+\beta)\right.\notag\\
 &\left.\hspace{13mm}+
 \zeta(1-\alpha-\beta)
       \left(\frac{2\pi mn}{t g^2}\right)^{\alpha+\beta}\right\}
                   \Phi(t/T)\dd t,\label{eq:kernel}\\
 \mathcal G_T(c,d;A,B)
 ={}&\sum_{m,n=1}^N c_m\overline{d_n}\mathcal K_T(m,n;A,B).\notag
\end{align}
Positive real bases in complex powers use the real logarithm.
These formulas are used only with $A+B\ne0$ and sufficiently large $T$.
At $A+B=0$ the raw bracket in Lean is formally
$2\,\text{\texttt{riemannZeta 1}}$, not the removable holomorphic extension.
No extension across that divisor is used in this proof.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform finite-family moment]\label{prop:family}
Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:weil} and~\ref{hyp:di}.
Fix $\Phi$ as above, $\tau>0$, $S\ge0$ and $r>0$.
There exist $C>0$ and $T_0$ such that, for every $T\ge T_0$,
every $\theta>0$, every integer $N\ge1$, all vectors
$z,w\in\mathbb C^7$ with $|z_i|,|w_i|\le2$, and all $A,B\in\mathbb C$
satisfying
\[
 N+1\le T,\quad N\le T^{4/7},\quad
 \log(T^\theta)\ge1,\quad N\le T^\theta,\qquad
 |A|,|B|\le S,\quad r\le|A+B|,
\]
the sequences $c=\sum_i z_i b_i(\theta,T)$ and
$d=\sum_i w_i b_i(\theta,T)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-bound}
 |\mathcal I_T(c,d;A,B)-\mathcal G_T(c,d;A,B)|
 \le C T^\tau\bigl(N^{7/4}+T^{1/2}N^{7/8}\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

This is the specialization of
\lean{PRZZFamilyMomentOptimal.moment_bound} obtained by fixing a smooth
monotone plateau seed and $K=4$. The seed equals $1$ on
$(-\infty,1/2]$, equals $0$ on $[1,\infty)$, and takes values in $[0,1]$.
The witness bridge constructs it from a smooth transition function.
The constant and height threshold precede $\theta,N,z,w,A,B$.
They may depend on the fixed seed, $\Phi,\tau,S,r$ and the input
constants. This is not a uniform assertion over arbitrary time weights
or arbitrary bounded coefficient sequences.

\subsection{Uniform smooth cutoffs and Mellin mass}

The support scale is not frozen when a profile is inserted into a
dyadic arithmetic cutoff. A useful intermediate class consists of
smooth $f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb C$, supported in $[a,b]$ with
$0<a\le b$, $\log(b/a)\le w$, and
\[
 |x^n f^{(n)}(x)|\le U_n\quad(n\ge0).
\]
For $\widehat f_{\!M}(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{s-1}\dd x$, the implemented
class estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin-class}
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+|v|)|\widehat f_{\!M}(\ii v)|\dd v
 \le4w(U_0+U_1+3U_2+U_3)
          \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\dd v}{1+v^2}.
\end{equation}
It follows from the logarithmic-coordinate derivative estimates and
integration by parts, with endpoint terms removed by smooth compact
support. The constants precede the function and the support scale.
\lean{PRZZProfileScale} supplies the dimensionless jets for the actual
height-dependent profiles, and \lean{PRZZProfileMellin.member_mass}
supplies \eqref{eq:mellin-class}. These bounds are carried through the
Type I/II and Heath--Brown block estimates before the outer row sums are
taken. A fixed-function bound alone would not justify
Proposition~\ref{prop:family}.

\subsection{What is assembled before the final bound}

The proof uses the actual Gamma-ratio AFE, not a replacement of its
kernel by definition. The moment error is split into the AFE replacement
error, the nonzero Poisson field, and the Gamma main-term defect.
The nonzero field is further split into its paired central body and its
body tail. The source cutoff, row Fourier cutoff and their transfer
bands are kept distinct until their correction terms are bounded.
Zero-frequency mismatch, spatial complements, boundary corrections,
freezing errors and large-scale terms are included before the
cutoffs are removed. Positive and negative frequencies and the two
dual branches are retained.

At the final assembly point the algebra has the form
\[
 E=(E-\mathcal R-\mathcal G_\Gamma)
       +(\mathcal R-\mathcal B)+\mathcal B+\mathcal G_\Gamma,
 \qquad E=\mathcal I_T-\mathcal G_T.
\]
The four suppliers used by
\lean{PRZZFamilyMomentOptimal.basis_bound} are respectively
\lean{PRZZCappedMainTerm.zero_removed_reduction},
\lean{PRZZCappedBodyTail.nonzero_reduction},
\lean{PRZZBasisPairedBody.bound}, and
\lean{PRZZCappedGammaDefect.mixed_absorbed}.
Here $\mathcal B$ is the actual paired central body, not an independent
proxy. The Weil and DI hypotheses enter its estimate. The preceding
reductions also include the Gamma-to-density kernel comparison
\lean{PRZZCappedMainTerm.mainterm_absorbed}.

For each of the 49 ordered basis pairs this gives a common upper bound
$U$. Sesquilinearity then gives a bound
\[
 \sum_{i,j}|z_i|\,|w_j|\,U\le196U.
\]
The coordinate vectors may vary with height. Polarization is available
as an algebraic bridge, but it does not replace the analytic uniformity
of the 49 basis estimates.

\subsection{Witness specialization and loss margins}

\lean{PRZZWitnessBridge.witness_estimate} supplies
$N=\lfloor T^\theta\rfloor$ for each fixed $0<\theta<4/7$, and identifies
the compact-window integral with the public all-real-time integral,
since $\Phi(t/T)$ vanishes outside $[T,2T]$.
Thus \lean{PRZZ.przz41_case3_K2_witness} is now a theorem with explicit
Weil/DI arguments, not a public analytic axiom.

Every $\tau>0$ is available. For the value of $\theta$ in
\eqref{eq:constants}, choosing $\tau=1/4000000$ gives
\[
 T^{\tau-1}\bigl(y^{7/4}+T^{1/2}y^{7/8}\bigr)
 =T^{-3/2000000}+T^{-1/1600000}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The public counting route uses the also admissible loss
$7/16000000$; its two normalized exponents are
$-21/16000000$ and $-7/16000000$. These are distinct choices in two
proved specializations. The smaller positive margin from the second
term must not be replaced by the first term's margin.

\subsection{Orientation of the shifts and the historical correction}

For the density in \eqref{eq:moment-pair}, a diagonal term obeys
$dm=em'$. Writing $g=(d,e)$ gives $m=(e/g)h$, $m'=(d/g)h$, and hence
$m^{-\alpha}(m')^{-\beta}=(e/g)^{-\alpha}(d/g)^{-\beta}
h^{-\alpha-\beta}$. The density kernel therefore places $\beta$ on
$d$ and $\alpha$ on $e$. The placement is essential for independent
left and right coefficients.

PRZZ Theorem~4.1 prints the opposite denominator \cite[p.~18]{PRZZ}.
The historical tag \lean{v0.1.0} copied that placement into a
general-profile input. The current source removes that axiom and proves
the witness input with the density-oriented kernel. For real profiles,
exchanging the two finite indices proves
$M(P,Q;A,B)=M(Q,P;B,A)$
(\lean{PRZZ.gcdMainTerm_swap}); thus the two main terms coincide for
the real witness self-pairing
(\lean{PRZZ.witness_densityGcdMainTerm_eq}).
This equality holds before limits or differentiation. Both ordered
cross terms and the identical operators on the two sides preserve the
numerical functional. It is not a justification of the old general
mixed-profile statement. The historical tag and its records remain
unchanged; a full check of the BCHB original is not claimed here.

\section{Arithmetic main terms and the finite functional}\label{sec:mainterm}

The gcd sum in \eqref{eq:kernel} is not defined to be the finite
functional below. Their asymptotic identification is a separate part
of the proof. The arithmetic calculation retains the unrestricted
Dirichlet convolution in \eqref{eq:coeff}, extracts its Euler factors
and leading poles, and estimates the remainder uniformly on the
shift region needed for differentiation.

For \(\ell,m\in\{0,2\}\), the four rational kernels are
\begin{align}
 K_{00}(X,Y,h)&=\frac{XY}{h},\notag\\
 K_{20}(X,Y,h)&=\frac{XY}{h^3}-\frac{2Y}{h^2}+\frac{Y}{Xh},\notag\\
 K_{02}(X,Y,h)&=\frac{XY}{h^3}-\frac{2X}{h^2}+\frac{X}{Yh},\notag\\
 K_{22}(X,Y,h)&=\frac{7XY}{h^5}-\frac{6(X+Y)}{h^4}
 +\frac{X/Y+Y/X+8}{h^3}\notag\\
 &\hspace{9mm}-\frac{2(X^{-1}+Y^{-1})}{h^2}+\frac1{XYh}.
 \label{eq:kernels}
\end{align}
Use \(F_0=P_1\), \(F_2=P_2\), and define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal D_1(A,w;F)&=\theta^{-1}F'(w/\theta)+AF(w/\theta),\\
 \mathcal D_0(A,w;F)&=F(w/\theta),\\
 \mathcal D_{-1}(A,w;F)&=\int_0^w e^{-Az}F((w-z)/\theta)\dd z.
\end{align*}
For each monomial \(cX^eY^fh^{-n}\) in \(K_{\ell m}\), replace it by
\[
 \frac{c}{\theta^{\ell+m}}\int_0^\theta
 \frac{v^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}
 \mathcal D_e(A,\theta-v;F_\ell)
 \mathcal D_f(B,\theta-v;F_m)\dd v.
\]
The resulting function is \(H_{\ell m}(A,B)\). Define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I(A,B)
 &=\sum_{\ell,m\in\{0,2\}}
 \frac{H_{\ell m}(A,B)-e^{-A-B}H_{\ell m}(-B,-A)}{A+B},
 \label{eq:functional}\\
 M_*&=\left.\Qop\mathcal I(A,B)\right|_{A=B=-R}.
 \label{eq:mstar}
\end{align}
The dual operation exchanges the two shifts as well as negating them.
The evaluation in \eqref{eq:mstar} is away from \(A+B=0\).

\begin{proposition}[The fixed smooth weighted moment]\label{prop:mainterm}
Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:weil} and~\ref{hyp:di}. For the fixed data
\eqref{eq:constants}--\eqref{eq:psi} and every fixed real smooth
\(\Phi\) supported in \([1,2]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-limit}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T
 \int_T^{2T}
 |U_T(\tfrac12-R/\log T+\ii t)
   \Psi_T(\tfrac12-R/\log T+\ii t)|^2\Phi(t/T)\dd t
 =M_*\int_1^2\Phi(u)\dd u .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Here is the separation of proof obligations in the implementation.
\lean{TwoPieceMainTerm.normalized_witness_uniform} identifies the
normalized arithmetic main term uniformly with the finite shift
functional. \lean{TwoPieceMoment.normalizedMoment_uniform} combines
this with the actual moment estimate. The theorem
\lean{TwoPieceMoment.normalizedMoment_exactEvaluator_tendsto}
transfers the needed independent left and right shift derivatives
through a Cauchy-integral argument. These include all jets through
order six on each side, not only derivatives along the diagonal.
A fixed bidisc about \((-R,-R)\), with radii sufficiently small,
stays away from \(A+B=0\). Coefficients are held fixed during these
shift derivatives; their height dependence is not differentiated.
Finally \lean{WitnessMomentBound.witnessMoment_tendsto} identifies
the exact evaluator with the real number in \eqref{eq:mstar} and
gives \eqref{eq:weighted-limit}.

The Gamma multiplier in the dual approximate functional equation
is handled before this identification; it is not replaced by its
leading height power by definition. The density-oriented gcd kernel,
the two branches, their zero-frequency correction and the remaining
Gamma defect are kept distinct until their stated estimates apply.
Likewise, defining the inverse operators \(\mathcal D_e\) does not
by itself justify inverse Mellin inversion. The convergence,
contour-shift and integration-order lemmas are part of the source
chain supplying the arithmetic identification.

\section{The numerical gate and its two verification levels}\label{sec:numeric}

All fixed polynomial coefficients and parameters are rational.
The Lean counting proof uses the deliberately conservative bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lean-gate}
 M_*\le1.128201<1.128202
       <\exp\{R(1-0.8845)\}.
\end{equation}
The first and the strict functional gate are proved in
\lean{FixedCertificate}; the positive counting cap
\(C_*=1.128202\) and its strict comparison with the exponential target
are supplied in \lean{WitnessMomentBound}.
The interval displayed next is an independent numerical check, not
the precision claimed for \eqref{eq:lean-gate}.

\begin{lemma}[Independent finite interval calculation]\label{lem:finite}
The interval program accompanying this manuscript encloses
\eqref{eq:mstar} and its comparison threshold as follows:
\begin{align*}
 1.12818163807203565629105
 &<M_*<1.12818163807203565629124,\\
 1.12820541062467513417945
 &<e^{R(1-0.8845)}<1.12820541062467513417947.
\end{align*}
These statements concern the fixed finite functional, not the
analytic moment or the external exponential-sum hypotheses.
\end{lemma}

The program \lean{paper/numerical_interval.py} uses rational
polynomial algebra and interval arithmetic for the exponential
pieces. After expansion, polynomial integrals are exact beta
integrals. For the truncated exponential in \(\mathcal D_{-1}\),
the normalized \(j\)-th shift-derivative remainder is bounded by
\[
 \frac{3C_F\theta^{1+j}}{j!(1+j)}
       \frac{(R\theta)^{J+1}}{(J+1)!}.
\]
Here \(C_F\) bounds the profile on \([0,1]\), and \(R\theta<1\).
At truncation order \(J=24\), the retained propagated error is
less than \(8.799\times10^{-23}\). Runs at precision/order pairs
\((40,20)\), \((45,24)\), \((55,24)\), and \((70,24)\) are retained in
the certificate, using \texttt{mpmath 1.3.0}. This Python arithmetic and its libraries
are independently reproducible but are not themselves formally
verified in Lean.

The kernel-checked proof of \eqref{eq:lean-gate} does not read or
trust that JSON certificate. The distinction matters: an independent
program can give more displayed digits without increasing the
logical precision required by the formal theorem. No numerical
optimization was performed for this revision.

\section{From a weighted moment to actual zero counts}\label{sec:counting}

The counting object is not merely the polynomial differential
operator \(U_T\). Put \(r(u)=q(1/2-u)\); direct rational algebra gives
\begin{align*}
 r(u)={}&0.09491351+0.997010185u+2.089866u^2\\
        &{}-2.29671034u^4+1.7701948u^6.
\end{align*}
For a fixed height parameter \(T\), define
\[
 \eta_T(s)=\sum_{j=0}^6r_j(\log T)^{-j}\xi^{(j)}(s),
 \qquad V_T(s)=\eta_T(s)/H(s).
\]
The symmetry of \(\xi\) makes its even derivatives real and its odd
derivatives purely imaginary on the critical line. Since \(r\) has
only one nonzero odd coefficient, the imaginary part of \(\eta_T\)
there is the nonzero real multiple
\(0.997010185\,(\log T)^{-1}\Ima\xi'(s)\).
This is the exact xi-prime interface used for argument variation.

Define
\[
 p_*=1-\frac{\log C_*}{R},\qquad C_*=1.128202.
\]
By \eqref{eq:lean-gate}, \(p_*>0.8845\).
Write \(\NC(T,2T]\) and \(\NT(T,2T]\) for the corresponding AF counts
in that half-open height window.

\begin{lemma}[Eventual dyadic count]\label{lem:count}
Under Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:weil} and~\ref{hyp:di}, for every real
\(c<p_*\) there is \(T_c\) such that every real \(T\ge T_c\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic}
 c\,\NT(T,2T]\le\NC(T,2T].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

This is the contract of \lean{WuProportion.dyadic_eventually}.
The following points describe how the analytic and geometric
conditions are actually supplied.

\paragraph{Exact \(V_T\) and the left-line logarithm.}
The source estimates \(V_T-U_T\) in the weighted second moment.
It uses nonnegative smooth time weights to pass from
\eqref{eq:weighted-limit} to the logarithmic budget, with a strict
reserve supplied by \(C_*>M_*\).
The return to the original left line is proved by reverse Fatou
with a dominating positive part and the regular \(H\) factor.
No unproved endpoint-tightness or counting-gate assumption is used.
On the right line the object estimated is the product
\(V_T\Psi_T=1+O((\log T)^{-1})\); the argument does not assume that
the mollifier alone tends uniformly to \(1\).

\paragraph{Safe inward contours.}
For fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\), the horizontal heights are chosen in
\[
 u\in((1+\delta)T,(1+\delta)T+1),\qquad
 v\in((2-\delta)T-1,(2-\delta)T).
\]
Actual finite zero sets, boundary nonvanishing and regularity
conditions are supplied for these contours. The retained critical-line
zeros are included in \((T,2T]\); discarded endpoint strips are not
identified with empty sets. The leading argument-variation budget is
\[
 \frac{(1-2\delta)T\log T}{2}
       \left(p_*-\frac{2\varepsilon}{R}\right),
\]
with the proved lower-order costs retained in the finite-height
inequality. Division by \(\pi\) gives a zero-count budget.
One chooses \(\delta\) and \(\varepsilon\) using \(c<p_*\), before
taking \(T\) sufficiently large.

\paragraph{The denominator and all terminal heights.}
The pinned AF Riemann--von Mangoldt result for the actual xi-prime
count yields
\(\NT(T,2T]/(T\log T)\to1/(2\pi)\).
The local count and this normalization imply \eqref{eq:dyadic}.
AF's additive dyadic-to-cumulative theorem then transfers the
inequality to every sufficiently large real terminal height \(X\).
Taking \(b=(p_*+0.8845)/2\) proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
This route does not replace the denominator by a zeta count, require
simple zeros, or assert a separately supplied arbitrary-window
counting adapter.
